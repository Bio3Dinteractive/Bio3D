Hibiscus biology essentials

Classification

Scientific name: Hibiscus rosa-sinensis

  • Kingdom Plantae
  • Phylum Angiosperms
  • Class Eudicots
  • Order Malvales
  • Family Malvaceae

Flower structure

The hibiscus flower is bisexual and contains both androecium and gynoecium. The five petals form the conspicuous corolla, while sepals form the calyx.

Androecium

Numerous stamens surround the central style. Their filaments are united into a staminal column, a condition called monadelphous. The anthers produce pollen grains.

Gynoecium

The gynoecium consists of the ovary, style and stigma. The ovary is superior and contains ovules arranged in locules with axile placentation.

Pollination

The colourful petals and floral form help attract pollinators. Pollen reaching the stigma can germinate and produce a pollen tube that grows through the style toward an ovule.

Fruit and seed formation

After fertilisation, ovules develop into seeds and the ovary develops into the fruit. The embryo develops within the seed.
