Frog biology essentials

Classification

Scientific name: Hoplobatrachus tigerinus (formerly Rana tigrina), the Indian frog used in many school biology references.

  • Kingdom Animalia
  • Phylum Chordata
  • Class Amphibia
  • Order Anura
  • Family Ranidae

Thermoregulation and circulation

Frogs are ectothermic and poikilothermic: body temperature depends largely on the environment. They are not “half-blooded”; their heart gives incomplete double circulation, because oxygenated and deoxygenated blood can partly mix in the single ventricle.

Respiration

Adults use three routes: cutaneous respiration through moist skin, pulmonary respiration through lungs, and buccopharyngeal respiration through the moist lining of the mouth and pharynx. Tadpoles primarily use branchial respiration through gills.

Special frog adaptations

The frog tongue is attached at the front of the mouth and is folded back over the floor of the buccal cavity when at rest. It flips outward rapidly to catch prey; sticky mucus traps insects, then elastic tissue and muscles retract the tongue.

The tympanum is the external eardrum. Raised eyes, a nictitating membrane, moist glandular skin, webbed hind feet, and powerful hindlimbs are adaptations for amphibious life.

The front limbs end in hands with digits that are generally much less webbed than the hind feet. The broad hind-foot webbing is the major webbed-foot adaptation used for swimming.

Reproduction

Fertilisation is generally external in water. Eggs pass through the female oviduct and are released through the cloaca; in males, sperm and urine share the urinogenital duct before reaching the cloaca.
